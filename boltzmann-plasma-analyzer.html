Understand every step

How one table becomes a temperature.

The tool is deliberately transparent. It shows the transformed values and regression instead of hiding the calculation behind a button.

  1. 01

    Transform each line

    For every row, calculate y = ln(Iλ/gA). This corrects the measured intensity for known transition properties.

  2. 02

    Fit y against Eu

    Ordinary least-squares regression finds the straight line that minimizes the squared vertical residuals.

  3. 03

    Read the negative slope

    When upper energy is measured in electronvolts, slope = −1/(kBT), using kB = 8.617333262 × 10⁻⁵ eV/K.

  4. 04

    Examine—not merely report—the fit

    Review R², energy spread, residuals, line selection and experimental assumptions before treating the estimate as meaningful.

Scientific conditions

When the estimate can mislead.

A calculated number can be precise on screen and still be scientifically unreliable. These checks matter as much as the regression.

  • Same emitting speciesUse transitions from one element and one ionization stage in a standard Boltzmann plot.
  • LTE approximationLevel populations must be reasonably described by a Boltzmann distribution at one excitation temperature.
  • Optically thin linesStrong self-absorption can reduce observed intensity and bend or scatter the plot.
  • Reliable atomic dataIncorrect Aki, gk or Eu values directly affect each plotted point.
  • Calibrated responseA detector may respond differently at different wavelengths; relative spectral response should be corrected.
  • Useful energy spreadLines spanning a wider range of upper energies constrain the slope better than tightly clustered energies.
  • Unblended integrated linesOverlapping peaks and inconsistent integration boundaries can distort intensity values.

Beginner glossary

The vocabulary behind the plot.

Emission line
Light at a characteristic wavelength released when an excited atom or ion moves to a lower energy level.
Integrated intensity, I
The area under an emission peak after background correction, in consistent arbitrary or calibrated units.
Transition probability, Aki
The probability per second that the upper state k decays radiatively to the lower state i.
Statistical weight, gk
The degeneracy of the upper energy level. NIST lists it as g = 2J + 1.
Upper energy, Eu
The energy of the emitting upper level relative to the ground level, entered here in electronvolts.
Natural logarithm, ln
A mathematical transformation that turns the exponential Boltzmann relationship into a straight-line equation.
Residual
The vertical difference between an observed plot point and the value predicted by the fitted line.
R²
A measure of how much variation is described by the fitted line. Closer to 1 is more linear, but not automatically more valid.
Excitation temperature
The temperature parameter describing relative excited-level populations under the model assumptions.
LTE
Local thermodynamic equilibrium: an approximation in which local particle populations can be described by thermodynamic relations.
