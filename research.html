Master's-level research case study · 2021

Comparative Nutritional Characterization of Healthy and Virus-Infected Plant Samples

Using laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy and complementary analytical methods to investigate elemental changes in cotton and pepper leaves.

01 / Overview

Why study plant health through light?

Plant disease can alter nutrient uptake and elemental composition before every change is obvious to the eye. Spectroscopy offers a route to examine these differences through the light emitted or absorbed by a sample.

This study compared healthy and virus-infected cotton and pepper leaves. LIBS provided rapid multi-element emission spectra with little sample preparation, while AAS supplied a complementary concentration measurement. CF-LIBS and PCA were then used to extend the quantitative and multivariate interpretation.

02 / Research question

Can complementary spectroscopic methods identify and characterize elemental differences between healthy and virus-infected cotton and pepper leaves?
01

Acquire elemental emission spectra from prepared healthy and infected samples.

02

Identify micro-, macro- and trace-element signatures using reference spectral data.

03

Compare concentration patterns with AAS and calibration-free LIBS.

04

Use PCA to examine variation and separation between sample groups.

03 / Methodology

From leaf collection to multivariate interpretation.

  1. 01

    Collect and clean

    Healthy and infected cotton and pepper leaves were collected at UAF and washed with distilled water.

  2. 02

    Dry and grind

    Leaves were dried for 24 hours in a solar dehydrator and ground into fine powder.

  3. 03

    Form pellets

    Approximately 0.5 g of powder was pressed into 15 mm pellets for consistent laser interaction.

  4. 04

    Generate plasma

    A focused Q-switched Nd:YAG pulse ablated the sample surface and produced an emitting plasma.

  5. 05

    Acquire spectra

    Optical emission was collected through fibre optics and recorded by a multichannel spectrometer.

  6. 06

    Analyze and compare

    OriginPro, NIST references, AAS, CF-LIBS and PCA were used to interpret elemental differences.

05 / Spectroscopy

Reading elemental signatures across the spectrum.

Peak positions were compared with NIST reference data. Peak fitting and integrated intensity supported plasma characterization and comparison between samples.

06 / Results and interpretation

Multiple methods pointed to measurable variation.

Elemental identification

LIBS spectra included macro- and micronutrient signatures such as Ca, Mg, Fe, Cu, Mn and Mo, alongside trace or potentially toxic elements reported in the thesis.

Quantitative comparison

AAS measured concentration differences between healthy and infected samples; CF-LIBS produced directionally comparable patterns for selected species.

Multivariate separation

For cotton, the first three principal components accounted for 98.31% of the reported variation; for pepper, they accounted for 96.04%.

Plasma validation

Boltzmann plots and Stark broadening were used to estimate temperature and electron density and assess LTE assumptions.

Research conclusion

The study found systematic elemental and intensity differences after infection and demonstrated how LIBS, AAS, CF-LIBS and PCA can work together for comparative plant analysis. Further validation on larger datasets would be required before claiming a diagnostic system.

08 / Research integrity

Clear boundaries between evidence and ambition.

This portfolio reports completed work from the archived thesis and supporting research material. It does not claim that the study produced a clinically or agriculturally deployable diagnostic system. Equipment details are included only where documented, and future interests—such as advanced photonics and machine learning—are presented as directions for development.
