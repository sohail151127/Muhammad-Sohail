Follow the calculation

The answer is a chain, not a black box.

Open each step to see the idea, the equation and the values currently being used.

01
Estimate the focused spotWavelength + focal length + beam width + beam quality

A lens bends the incoming beam toward a waist. Longer focal lengths, longer wavelengths and poorer beam quality produce a larger estimated spot; a wider beam at the lens produces a smaller spot.

d = 4 M² λ f ÷ (π D)

—

02
Estimate peak powerEnergy divided by time

The same pulse energy produces more peak power when it is delivered in a shorter time. This tool uses the simple rectangular-equivalent estimate.

Ppeak ≈ E ÷ τ

—

03
Estimate Gaussian peak fluencePulse energy concentrated into the focused area

A Gaussian beam is brightest at its centre. For a 1/e² radius w, its on-axis peak fluence includes a factor of two.

Fpeak = 2E ÷ (πw²)

—

04
Estimate Gaussian peak irradiancePeak power concentrated into the focused area

Irradiance describes optical power arriving per unit area. The estimate uses the calculated peak power and Gaussian beam area.

Ipeak = 2Ppeak ÷ (πw²)

Read before interpreting

What the model assumes.

Real laser systems are more complicated. Stating assumptions makes an estimate scientifically honest and helps prevent false precision.

  • Collimated inputThe beam diameter is treated as nearly constant before the lens.
  • Gaussian spatial profileThe diameter is the full 1/e² intensity width, not an arbitrary visible edge.
  • Ideal thin lensLens aberrations, clipping and imperfect alignment are not modelled.
  • Energy at the sampleLosses from mirrors, air and lens transmission are not automatically removed.
  • Simple temporal estimatePeak power uses energy divided by pulse duration; the exact temporal pulse shape may change the true peak.
  • No plasma feedbackPlasma shielding, nonlinear propagation and sample interaction are outside this first-order model.

Beginner glossary

Every symbol, in ordinary language.

λ · Wavelength
The spatial period of light. This calculator accepts nanometres (nm).
E · Pulse energy
Energy contained in one pulse, entered in millijoules (mJ).
τ · Pulse duration
The time length of one pulse, entered in nanoseconds (ns).
D · Input diameter
The full 1/e² beam diameter where the collimated beam reaches the lens.
f · Focal length
A lens property that controls how strongly the beam is focused.
M² · Beam quality
A measure of how closely a real beam behaves like an ideal Gaussian beam.
1/e² diameter
A standard Gaussian-beam width. At its radius, the intensity has fallen to about 13.5% of the centre value.
w · Focused radius
Half the calculated 1/e² spot diameter at the beam waist.
Fluence
Pulse energy delivered per unit area, usually written in J/cm².
Irradiance
Optical power delivered per unit area, usually written in W/cm².
