Beginner guide

What the three shapes mean.

No line profile proves a mechanism on its own. These are compact mathematical descriptions of the measured peak.

01

Gaussian

A bell-shaped profile with relatively light wings. Doppler and many instrumental contributions are commonly Gaussian-like.

02

Lorentzian

A profile with broader wings. Collision and pressure effects, including Stark broadening under suitable conditions, are commonly treated as Lorentzian-like.

03

Pseudo-Voigt

A weighted mixture of Gaussian and Lorentzian profiles sharing one centre and FWHM. It is a practical approximation, not an exact physical separation.

04

FWHM

Full width at half maximum: the wavelength distance between the two locations where the fitted peak reaches half its fitted height.

Scientific boundaries

Before reporting a width.

  • Fit one isolated, unsaturated peak with enough points across both sides.
  • Inspect the baseline and residual plot; a high R² alone does not validate a model.
  • The displayed uncertainty describes local least-squares precision only. Add wavelength calibration, resolution, repeatability and model-choice uncertainty separately.
  • For Stark analysis, correct instrumental and other non-Stark broadening and use an appropriate published parameter.
