Visible mathematics

Exactly what was calculated.

Calibration line: Signal = intercept + slope × concentration

Unknown: Concentration = (unknown signal − intercept) ÷ slope

Detection limit: LOD = 3.3σ ÷ |slope|

Quantitation limit: LOQ = 10σ ÷ |slope|

Beginner guide

Four terms that prevent confusion.

These results describe the entered calibration dataset. They do not automatically validate an analytical method.

01

Calibration standard

A sample with a known analyte concentration, prepared and measured to connect concentration with instrument response.

02

Residual

The difference between a measured signal and the signal predicted by the line. Residuals reveal patterns hidden by R².

03

LOD and LOQ

LOD is an estimated detection limit; LOQ is an estimated quantitation limit. Both require experimental confirmation near the lower range.

04

R² is not validation

A value near one describes linear agreement in this dataset. It does not prove accuracy, selectivity, precision, matrix matching or absence of self-absorption.

Scientific boundaries

Before reporting concentration.

  • Use matrix-matched standards and the same acquisition and preprocessing conditions as unknown samples.
  • Confirm linearity across the working range and investigate self-absorption, spectral interference and changing plasma conditions.
  • Do not report an extrapolated unknown as reliable without extending and validating the calibration range.
  • The displayed 95% interval is a regression-based estimate; it excludes preparation, reference-standard, matrix and instrument uncertainties.
  • Validate the proposed LOQ using accuracy and precision measurements near that concentration.
