Calculation used—

Understand every step

From a peak to Ne.

The calculation is short, but the scientific choices around it decide whether the result is meaningful.

  1. 01

    Fit the observed peak

    Measure FWHM from a background-corrected, isolated emission line. FWHM is the horizontal width of the peak at half its maximum height.

  2. 02

    Estimate plasma width

    Remove instrumental broadening using the selected approximation. The corrected value is treated as the Stark FWHM only when other broadening contributions are small.

  3. 03

    Standardize the reference

    If a source reports electron-impact half-width w, the corresponding reference FWHM is 2w. If it already reports full width, no factor of two is added.

  4. 04

    Scale to electron density

    For a suitable isolated non-hydrogen line, width is approximately linear with electron density: Ne = Nref(ΔλStark/Δλref).

Scientific conditions

Check these before reporting.

A neat answer on screen cannot rescue an unsuitable line or an incorrect reference parameter.

  • Not a hydrogen lineThis simple linear calculator is for suitable isolated non-hydrogen lines. Hydrogen and hydrogen-like lines require specialized profiles or empirical relations.
  • Isolated and unblendedNearby transitions can make one apparent peak wider than the line being studied.
  • Instrument characterizedMeasure instrumental width with a narrow calibration source near the wavelength of interest.
  • Other widths assessedDoppler, resonance and van der Waals broadening should be estimated rather than automatically assigned to Stark broadening.
  • Optical depth checkedSelf-absorption can change both the shape and width of a strong emission line.
  • Correct atomic parameterThe reference width must belong to the same transition and use a compatible temperature and density convention.
  • Approximation documentedGaussian quadrature and Lorentzian subtraction are simplified width corrections, not complete profile deconvolution.

Beginner glossary

The vocabulary behind line width.

Stark broadening
Widening of a spectral line caused by electric fields and collisions involving charged particles in a plasma.
Electron density, Ne
The number of free electrons per unit volume, reported here as electrons per cubic centimetre (cm⁻³).
FWHM
Full width at half maximum: the wavelength distance between the two sides of a peak at half of its maximum height.
Half-width, w
Half of a full line width. Some literature reports w, while databases such as STARK-B report full width; confusing them creates a factor-of-two error.
Instrumental broadening
Line width introduced by the spectrometer, slit, detector and optical system rather than by the plasma.
Lorentzian profile
A line-shape model with a narrow centre and extended wings, commonly associated with collision broadening.
Gaussian profile
A bell-shaped line model often used to approximate instrumental or Doppler broadening.
Deconvolution
The process of separating the instrument response from the physical line profile.
Isolated line
A transition sufficiently separated from neighbouring lines for its width and shape to be studied independently.
Self-absorption
Reabsorption of emitted light by cooler atoms of the same species, which can distort a spectral line.
Where to obtain and verify Stark data

STARK-B provides calculated full widths and shifts for isolated lines at stated temperatures and perturber densities. NIST explains physical and instrumental contributions to spectral-line shapes. The linked LIBS review presents the linear non-hydrogen relation used here.
